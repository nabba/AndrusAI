# High-Fidelity Evidence Mapping and Triangulation

## Purpose
To ensure maximum accuracy and reliability when synthesizing information from multiple disparate sources. This skill prevents "hallucination by aggregation" and identifies gaps or contradictions in research.

## Methodology: The Triangulation Framework

### 1. Source Inventory & Weighting
- **Catalog**: List every unique source used for a specific claim.
- **Weighting**: Assign a reliability score (1-5) based on:
    - Authority (Peer-reviewed > Official Docs > Reputable News > Blog/Social)
    - Recency (Date of publication vs. current date)
    - Bias (Neutral/Technical vs. Promotional/Opinionated)

### 2. Evidence Mapping (The Matrix)
Create a mental or physical matrix where:
- **Rows**: Specific claims or data points.
- **Columns**: Sources.
- **Cells**: [Support | Contradict | Neutral | No Mention]

### 3. Conflict Resolution Protocol
When sources contradict:
- **Preference**: Trust the source with the highest weight.
- **Nuance**: If weights are equal, report the contradiction as a "point of uncertainty" rather than picking one.
- **Synthesis**: Look for a third source to break the tie (The Tie-Breaker Rule).

### 4. Confidence Scoring
Assign a final confidence level to the synthesized conclusion:
- **High**: 3+ independent high-weight sources agree.
- **Medium**: 2 high-weight sources agree, or 3+ low-weight sources agree.
- **Low**: Only one source available, or high-weight sources contradict.

## Application Guide
Use this skill when the user asks for:
- "The most accurate" or "definitive" answer.
- Comparative analysis of different viewpoints.
- Technical specifications where precision is critical.
- Research on emerging topics with conflicting reports.
